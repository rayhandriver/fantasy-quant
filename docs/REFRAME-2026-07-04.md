## 1. TL;DR

The project pivots from *"build a model that beats consensus ADP"* to *"build a personalization engine
that gives the user the team they want, and honestly prices what that costs versus optimal."* This is the
**direct-indexing philosophy** from the AlphaThena internship, applied to fantasy drafting.

The old objective (beat ADP, prove it with bootstrap CIs) is replaced. Team strength stops being the
**objective** and becomes a **benchmark we track against with a tolerance budget.** Personalization
becomes the objective. This is a deliberate inversion, and it resolves the single biggest risk in the
original plan (see Section 3).

AI-in-app features are **deferred** to post-MVP (see Section 9). The near-term build is a fully functional,
deterministic personalization app with no LLM dependency.

## 2. The core reframe (direct indexing for fantasy)

AlphaThena never tries to beat the S&P 500. It tracks the benchmark as closely as possible (minimize
tracking error) while layering on value that requires no market-beating skill: tax-loss harvesting (small,
systematic, compounding wins) and personalization (exclude sectors, tilt to values, trim concentrated
positions). It is a constrained optimization: **maximize personalization / tax-alpha subject to tracking
error staying within tolerance.** The tool does the heavy lifting; the human stays in control.

Direct one-to-one mapping to fantasy:

| AlphaThena | Fantasy-Quant |
|---|---|
| S&P 500 benchmark | The "optimal" team the engine would draft from your seat |
| Tracking error (drift from index) | Projected value / championship-equity gap vs that optimal team |
| Custom index (exclude, tilt, ESG) | Must-draft, never-draft, over/under-weight, fandom, strategy archetype |
| Tax-loss harvesting (small compounding wins) | In-season start/sit, streaming, waivers tuned to weekly matchup |
| Maximize tax alpha s.t. tracking error ≤ tolerance | Maximize user preferences s.t. team-strength cost ≤ user tolerance |
| Advisor stays in control | User decides; model informs and prices tradeoffs |

**The signature metric** falls out of this: a **cost-of-personalization report** (the tracking-error
decomposition). Example: *"Locking Bijan in round 1 and refusing to draft Kelce cost you an estimated
12-18 PAR and ~2% championship probability versus the optimal build from your seat. Here is the
per-decision breakdown."* No existing fantasy tool does this. Rankings tools tell you the "right" pick and
fight you when you disagree. This one lets you indulge freely and prices the indulgence honestly.

### 2.1 The three signal layers (value / availability / variance)

**The reframe still outputs a single, fully optimized personalized team.** It does not mean "give a worse
team." It means we stop *claiming* our team beats consensus, and we measure personalization cost against
the consensus-optimal team. The optimizer is still building the best roster it can given everything the
user wants.

"Based around ADP" must be split into **three distinct signals**, because ADP is being asked to do jobs
that are not all ADP. Collapsing them into one undifferentiated "ADP" input produces an incoherent
optimizer.

1. **Value — what makes one team better than another → consensus projections, run through VBD.**
   This is **not** ADP. ADP is a draft-*order* signal, not a points estimate; you cannot compute "best
   team," or "avoiding Kelce cost you 12 PAR," from draft order alone. Use consensus projected points
   (e.g. FantasyPros aggregate) as the mean value signal, and always convert to **value-over-replacement
   (VBD)**, never raw consensus order, because raw order overrates QBs in a 1-QB league (our own Phase-2
   finding). This is the big simplification the reframe buys: lean on consensus for the mean and drop
   most of the "build a better projection" burden.

2. **Availability — who is gettable at each pick → ADP plus the behavioral model.**
   This was always ADP and stays ADP. It drives "who survives to my next pick" and the whole draft-flow
   plan (features d and e).

3. **Variance / risk — floor vs ceiling → our own distributional layer.**
   Consensus and ADP are both point estimates and cannot hand you distributions. The per-round risk dial
   needs a variance or full distribution per player, which must come from our own modeling (a trimmed
   Phase 5). Required only if the risk features are in scope.

**Optimizer objective:** maximize **consensus-VBD value** subject to the user's constraints and archetype,
planning around **ADP-driven availability**, with the **risk dial** modulating the value/variance
tradeoff. Personalization cost is priced against the consensus-VBD-optimal team.

Keep two things straight so the build does not tangle: **value is consensus *projections*, not ADP
*order*** (they are correlated but not the same object), and **the risk dial still needs a variance model
we build ourselves.**

## 3. Why the reframe matters (the problem it solves)

The original definition of done required proving a statistically significant edge over ADP on a
walk-forward. That is very likely **unachievable with the available data**, and this was demonstrated from
the project's own numbers:

- Finding 1 (naive model vs ADP): edge −59 PAR/season, 95% CI **[−162, +33]**. Half-width ≈ 98 implies a
  standard error on the mean per-season difference of ≈ **50 PAR**.
- With ≈ 9-10 evaluation seasons, the per-season difference SD ≈ 50 × √10 ≈ **150 PAR**.
- Power requirement: n ≥ (2.8 × SD / δ)². To detect an edge of δ = 80 PAR/season at 80% power needs
  ≈ **27 seasons**; δ = 120 needs ≈ 12; δ = 50 needs ≈ 70. We have ≈ 10.
- Empirical confirmation: the best in-sample blend was +80 PAR and its CI **still crossed zero**
  [−22, +190]. The harness has already shown it cannot resolve an 80-PAR edge, and 80 PAR (~4% over the
  ~2036 ADP base) is a *large* edge for a projection to have over market consensus.

**Conclusion:** the original "prove you beat ADP" finish line is probably not reachable, even if the model
is genuinely good. The direct-indexing reframe removes the need to win that fight. The benchmark (optimal
team) is something we **build, not beat**, and the product's value (cost of a preference, availability of a
player, calibration of a risk dial) is measurable **within a single draft**, with no bootstrap needed. We
trade an unwinnable inferential fight for a set of well-posed, checkable prediction problems. This is the
same reason AlphaThena's business works without alpha.

**New definition of done (v1):** a league-usable, season-long personalization co-pilot that (a) produces
**well-calibrated** projections and risk dials, (b) accurately predicts **draft availability**, and (c)
honestly **prices the cost of each personalization decision** against one or more benchmarks. "Beat ADP"
is demoted from a requirement to an optional, secondary curiosity.

## 4. Methodological cautions to carry forward

These were raised in review and remain valid under the reframe. Claude Code should record them as
explicit constraints/findings.

1. **PIT-cleanliness ≠ out-of-sample cleanliness.** The existing discipline prevents temporal leakage but
   not backtest-overfitting from repeatedly selecting models/features on the same ~10 seasons. **Carve out
   a true lockbox** (e.g. freeze the two most recent affordable seasons, never touch during development,
   evaluate the final chosen stack there exactly once). This still applies to calibration and availability
   claims under the reframe.
2. **Calibration > edge.** Under the reframe the projections no longer need to beat ADP; they need to be
   well-calibrated (reliability diagrams, prediction-interval coverage). This is a lower and far more
   provable bar, and it is what the risk dials and cost estimates depend on. A miscalibrated projection
   now becomes a **visibly wrong number the user sees**, so calibration rigor matters *more*, not less.
3. **Objective is a mean/tail question.** Season-total PAR is a mean metric; championship probability is a
   tail metric. The reframe converts this into a user-facing feature (a risk / "championship-or-bust vs
   make-the-playoffs" toggle), so **both** objectives must be built. Also note the walk-forward's
   perfect-hindsight optimal-lineup scoring systematically rewards high-variance bench players; keep this
   in mind so it does not silently confound the distributional work.
4. **Rookies must not punt to ADP.** A last-year model cannot see rookies, but rookie RB/WR pricing is one
   of the richest sources of ADP mispricing, and the Phase-3 features (draft capital, landing spot,
   athletic/college profile) are exactly the rookie-projection inputs. Build an explicit rookie projection
   model. A user who loves a rookie needs an honest number, not an ADP fallback.
5. **FFCalculator ADP is soft money, not a sharp market.** The sharpest preseason signal is real-money
   best-ball ADP (e.g. Underdog). Part of any apparent edge over FFC ADP may just be FFC softness. Under
   the reframe this matters for the **benchmark** (use the best available) and for **availability
   prediction** (does the ADP predict how the user's league type actually drafts).
6. **Benchmark is self-referential.** Unlike the S&P 500, "optimal team" is defined by our own projections.
   The cost report is therefore "cost relative to what our model thinks is optimal." Mitigate by offering
   cost against **multiple benchmarks** (ADP-consensus-optimal, our-projection-optimal, expert-consensus-
   optimal).
7. **Cost-number uncertainty.** The cost figure inherits all projection and simulation uncertainty and
   its honest interval may be wide. **Lead with relative and directional costs** (this choice vs that
   choice), which are robust; stay humble on absolute championship-equity deltas.

## 5. Phase-by-phase impact

**Stays, and gets more important:**

- **Phase 3 (features)** — unchanged as the engine.
- **Phase 4 (mean value)** — **lean on consensus projections (VBD), not our own edge-seeking model** (see
  2.1, layer 1). The bar drops from "beat ADP" to "well-calibrated / faithful to consensus." Most of the
  original "build a better projection" burden goes away. Still add an explicit **rookie model** (see 4.4),
  since consensus rookie value is itself thin and a user who loves a rookie needs an honest number.
- **Phase 5 (variance / distributions)** — **promoted to core, and it is the one value-side thing we still
  build ourselves** (see 2.1, layer 3). Consensus and ADP are point estimates; the per-round risk dial
  needs a per-player variance/distribution that only our modeling provides. There is no high-vs-low-risk
  feature without it. Trim to what the risk dial actually needs.
- **Phase 8 (covariance)** — **promoted / earlier.** Tracking error is a covariance quantity, and
  correlation appetite becomes a user dial.
- **Phase 10 (season sim → championship probability)** — **promoted / earlier.** It is the benchmark we
  track against and the tail objective.

**Reframed:**

- **Phase 6 (ADP-bias mining)** — no longer "find edges to beat ADP." It becomes **personalization
  intelligence**: knowing where ADP is soft tells the app how cheaply a user can indulge a preference
  ("good news, you can wait a round on your guy").
- **Phase 11 (opponent modeling)** — the opponent model is **re-justified on measurable grounds**: its job
  is predicting draft flow (who survives to each pick), which has hard ground truth scorable with a Brier
  score over thousands of picks. It moves from unverifiable frontier flex to core, verifiable
  infrastructure. **Drop CFR** (a snake draft is near-perfect-information; CFR is a category error here).
  **Deprioritize heavy live MCTS** (unverifiable vs good greedy value-based drafting, and a latency risk
  in a live draft). Auctions are a separate format (later).
- **Phase 7 (causal "player-in-system")** — reframe as **opportunity-adjusted projection** and drop the
  causal counterfactual claims; they are not identifiable from observational NFL data. Useful as a feature,
  oversold as causal inference.

**New (not really in the current plan):**

- **A preference-specification layer** (the "custom index builder": constraint object + UI).
- **A constrained draft optimizer** — maximize projected value subject to hard constraints (never draft X)
  and soft tilts (prefer X by ~half a round), within a tracking-error budget. Direct analog of the
  AlphaThena optimizer. This is a real change to the Phase-9 policy.
- **The cost-of-personalization report** (tracking-error decomposition).
- **A behavioral opponent model** replacing "ADP + Gaussian noise": positional runs, reaches for
  favorites, hometown bias, rookie hype, name-brand bias, K/DST panic, handcuff behavior. Powers both the
  realistic mock (feature e) and availability forecasts (feature d).
- **Per-pick availability distributions** ("who is likely at 15, 26, 35, with probabilities"): survival
  modeling over simulated draft flow.

## 6. Personalization system design

### 6.1 One config object, three doors

There is a **single constraint/config object** under the hood. Three levels of access render the same
object at different depths, so it is not three apps, it is one config exposed at three levels:

- **Autopilot** — two decisions (archetype + league), then go.
- **Co-pilot** — five to seven high-impact dials exposed.
- **Manual** — the full surface, every slider.

### 6.2 Sliders everywhere, but never a blank form

Sliders are the preferred control in every applicable place. To avoid overwhelming casual users, **every
configurable resolves through a precedence chain** so nothing is ever unset:

```
explicit user setting  >  inferred from league settings  >  implied by chosen archetype  >  population prior
```

The optimizer always receives a **complete** config; the user never has to fill anything in. Three
supporting rules:

1. **Read the league, do not ask.** Scoring, roster slots, superflex, league size, and draft slot are
   synced or pasted once and automatically set replacement levels, positional priorities, and QB
   weighting. The tedious settings are exactly the inferable ones.
2. **The archetype is a master dial.** Choosing "Zero RB" or "balanced" cascades defaults into positional
   timing, per-round risk, and correlation appetite. One choice configures ~20 knobs; opening any knob
   just reveals what the preset pre-filled, editable.
3. **Surface the default with a one-line why, always overridable.** e.g. *"Set to Ceiling because you
   flagged this as a money league. Change it?"* Never a blank default, never a silent one. This is the
   AlphaThena move: the tool made a reasonable call, showed its work, left the user in control.

### 6.3 The signature seven (the spine to build around)

1. Preference-spec layer (constraint object + UI).
2. Constrained optimizer.
3. Cost-of-personalization report.
4. Behavioral opponent model → availability forecasts (d) + realistic mock (e).
5. Per-round / global risk dial (powered by Phase 5 distributions).
6. Adaptive archetypes (presets that abandon the plan when the board breaks).
7. In-season weekly-edge harvester (the tax-loss-harvesting analog).

## 7. Full personalization feature catalog

**Player / roster level:** must-draft and never-draft lists with a per-player "reach budget"; over/under-
weight sliders in units of rounds; team-fandom and anti-fandom modes (people refuse to draft a rival's
players); character / off-field exclusions (the ESG analog); injury-averse mode down-weighting high
re-injury-hazard players (the concentrated-position analog); rookie-lover vs veteran-lover tilt;
handcuff-your-own-RBs toggle.

**Strategy archetypes (feature b), as selectable templates:** Zero RB, Hero RB, Hero WR, Robust RB,
Elite/premium TE, late-round QB, Konami mobile-QB build, best-player-available purist. Allow blending two
with a slider. Offer an **adaptive** archetype that abandons the plan when the board breaks (if elite RBs
fall, drop Zero RB). Adaptive is the one most worth the quant.

**Risk (feature c):** per-round floor-vs-ceiling dial; a global profile (casual league = don't embarrass me
= floor; money league = win it all = ceiling); a correlation-appetite dial (stacked all-or-nothing vs
decorrelated steady, mapping to the covariance work); championship-or-bust vs make-the-playoffs objective
toggle.

**League context:** full custom scoring ingestion; roster settings including **superflex / 2QB** (very
popular now and it changes everything); league size; keeper/dynasty holds; draft slot and format (snake,
linear, third-round reversal, auction); auction budget + nomination strategy as a separate mode;
playoff-week targeting (favor players with strong Week 15-17 matchups, the "target date" analog).

**Draft flow / live:** availability forecasts per pick (d); positional-run alerts ("a run started, your RB
window is closing"); forward-looking "if you take X now, here is who is likely left at your next pick";
realistic mock sim (e) with configurable opponent personalities (mock against a homer league, a sharp
league, an ADP-robot league); post-draft three-way comparison of the team you got vs the team the model
wanted vs the team you asked for.

**In-season (the tax-loss-harvesting analog, closest structural match):** start/sit tuned to whether you
need a floor or a ceiling *this week* given your matchup (small compounding wins); FAAB bid aggressiveness
tuned to roster holes and league tendencies; streaming for QB/TE/DST/K at your risk level; trade
suggestions that respect "never trade my favorites"; sell-high / buy-low alerts (the rebalancing analog).

**Meta / UX:** explanation-depth toggle (just the pick / the reasoning / the full math); co-pilot-to-
autopilot control spectrum; saveable, shareable personalization profiles (your "custom index"), which could
seed a community template library.

## 8. Engineering & cost prognosis

**Money is not the constraint; time is.** Dev tooling is sunk cost (Claude Code already in use). A
personal / small-user deployment runs near zero: static frontend on a free tier, Python backend on a cheap
or free tier, DuckDB is just a file, nflverse data is free, and the only paid item (betting props) is
already flagged optional. Realistic monthly run cost is close to nothing until real traffic appears.

**The real cost is lumpy effort in four places** that stall solo AI-assisted projects, none of which are
about writing code: integration (components that do not talk correctly), non-local debugging (bug shows up
far from its cause), deployment (works locally, breaks in the cloud), and architectural drift (the AI
cheerfully adds inconsistency over months if nobody curates). Budget time for those, not for typing.

**"I will not understand the minute details" is fine, within a line.**
- Fine to delegate fully: React internals, exact optimizer implementation, CSS, ORM plumbing.
- Must retain: the **data contracts between components** (the constraint object, the projection output
  shape), enough to **verify an output is correct**, and enough to **isolate which component a bug lives
  in**. The one added discipline: **own the interfaces even while delegating the interiors.** If the AI
  owns the contracts, they drift and you will not notice until several things break at once.

**Scope honesty.** The full 15-phase plan plus reframe plus a production web app (live draft sync, auth)
is a multi-person, months-to-years build. Solo and part-time, the whole vision is not a near-horizon
"finish it" project. The move is: build the spine, ship a thin end-to-end slice, thicken it. A
personalization core + cost report + basic draft assistant is a coherent MVP that needs **none** of Phases
11-15.

**The single highest-leverage engineering decision:** do **not** build FastAPI + Next.js to use this
yourself. That production stack is the part least suited to the reliance-on-Claude model and it eats the
most time. Build the first real interface in **Streamlit or Gradio** (Python-fluent, working interactive
UI with sliders, upload, live output in days, zero frontend engineering). Validate whether the
personalization idea is good before spending months on a real frontend. The Next.js build becomes a later
"I have users and want polish" step, not a "so I can try it" step. This one choice likely cuts effort to a
fraction and removes the weakest area from the critical path.

**Verdict:** very doable as a spine + thin vertical slice; the reliance-on-Claude model is fine for that
as long as you own the contracts and keep verifying. The full polished app is a long haul, and you never
need the full thing to have something real and useful. The failure mode to avoid is not "writing code you
do not fully understand," it is letting the architecture get away from you while heads-down generating
features. Keep a rough system map and update it as you go.

## 9. AI-in-app features — DEFERRED (post-MVP)

**Decision: defer all in-app AI features until there is a fully functional, deterministic app.** They are
recorded here for later, not for the near-term scope. Claude Code should **not** build these yet and
should **not** introduce any LLM dependency into the core.

Guiding principle when they are eventually built: **AI on the edges, deterministic core.** The LLM only
ever (a) turns fuzzy human input into a validated structured object on the way in, or (b) turns structured
numeric output into human narrative on the way out. It never computes a number that must be correct. Each
AI spot is then a contained "prompt + JSON schema + validation" unit, addable/swappable without touching
the engine.

Future spots, in value-per-effort order (for reference only):
1. Natural-language intake (fuzzy description → validated constraint object → pre-filled sliders).
2. Recommendation narration (structured optimizer output → plain-English rationale; the explanation-depth
   toggle made real).
3. News/injury extraction (beat-writer text → structured timestamped event; the model prices impact, the
   LLM only extracts the fact). This is the existing Phase 12.
4. Post-draft / in-season narrative (team story, cost report write-up, waiver/trade rationale, drafted
   trade messages).
5. Draft-room copilot chat (tool-using agent over the existing deterministic functions; the flagship,
   build last).

## 10. Open decisions to resolve

1. **Human draft data (highest priority).** The behavioral opponent model and availability forecasts
   (features d, e) need a corpus of **real completed human drafts** (pick-by-pick sequences, not just
   ADP averages). Sleeper's API exposes completed drafts. Without this, d and e collapse back to
   ADP-plus-noise, which is exactly what the reframe is trying to move past. **Confirm this data is
   obtainable before committing to the behavioral model.**
2. **Paid props line.** Preseason betting-market data remains a paid gap. Under the reframe it is even
   more optional (we are not trying to beat the market). Decide explicitly whether to ever cross the paid
   line; if not, formally demote "beat the betting market" from the goals.
3. **Benchmark set.** Decide which benchmarks the cost report is measured against (recommend offering
   several: ADP-consensus-optimal, our-projection-optimal, expert-consensus-optimal).
4. **Lockbox seasons.** Choose which recent seasons to freeze as the untouched final-evaluation set.

## 11. Recommended near-term MVP scope

A coherent first slice that needs none of Phases 11-15 and no in-app AI:

- Reuse Phases 0-2 as-is (data, harness, baselines, projection → VBD → rank_fn).
- Wire the **three signal layers** (see 2.1) as separate inputs to the optimizer, not one "ADP" blob:
  - **Value:** ingest **consensus projections** and run them through **VBD**. No own edge-seeking model.
    Add a thin rookie-value fill since consensus rookie value is sparse.
  - **Availability:** ADP (start with the existing ADP + noise simulator; upgrade to the behavioral model
    once real draft data is confirmed, decision 10.1).
  - **Variance:** a minimal own per-player distribution (trimmed Phase 5), only enough to drive a
    floor/ceiling risk dial.
- The **constraint object** (Section 6) + a **constrained greedy optimizer** maximizing **consensus-VBD
  value** subject to hard excludes, soft tilts, archetype priors, and the per-round risk dial, planning
  around ADP availability.
- A first-pass **cost-of-personalization** number vs the **consensus-VBD-optimal** team (relative /
  directional first).
- A **Streamlit / Gradio** interface exposing the Autopilot + Co-pilot tiers (archetype, league sync,
  a handful of dials, must/never lists) with defaulted sliders and the "one-line why."

Definition of MVP done: you can sit down for a real draft this season, express your preferences, and get
personalized pick recommendations with an honest, relative cost readout, from a Python UI, with no LLM in
the loop.
